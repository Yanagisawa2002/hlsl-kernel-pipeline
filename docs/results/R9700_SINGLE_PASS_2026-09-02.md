# R9700 persistent single-pass scan evidence — 2026-09-02

## Outcome

The reusable scan workload now has three interchangeable HLSL backends:

1. hierarchical Blelloch block scan plus recursive sums and offset propagation;
2. wave-hybrid local scan with the same global hierarchy;
3. persistent single-pass scan with atomic logical-block claiming and decoupled
   look-back.

Backend 3 is independent of Unity and of the particle showcase. It consumes the
public raw-buffer ABI, is selectable entirely through manifest defines, and has
its own `manifests/scan-single-pass.json` tuning surface.

## Device and rules

- GPU: AMD Radeon AI PRO R9700
- Driver: 32.0.31041.1004
- Backend / shader model: D3D12 / SM 6.0
- Compiler integration: Vortice.Dxc 3.8.3.0
- OS: Windows 10.0.26200
- Warm-up: at least 75 ms per candidate
- Measurements: 15 batches, calibrated complete-plan repetitions
- Stability: CV ≤ 5% for both baseline and selected result
- Correctness: independent CPU oracle and full verified-buffer SHA-256
- Static evidence: RGA was disabled for these runs; no register or occupancy
  claim is made here

The single-pass epoch/reset dispatch is included in every timestamped plan. GPU
upload, readback, hashing, GIF encoding, and CPU composition remain excluded.

## Pure 4M scan

The normal three-backend manifest expanded 36 candidates. All 36 were correct
and stable.

| Result | Configuration | Median | P95 | vs declared baseline |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline | Blelloch, group 256, EPT 4 | 0.052315 ms | 0.052931 ms | 1.0000x |
| Selected | Single-pass, group 512, IPT 8, 256 persistent groups | 0.043158 ms | 0.043508 ms | 1.2122x |

The backend-specific 16M tuner evaluated 48 combinations of group size, item
scale, and persistent-group limit. All 48 were correct. It selected group 512,
IPT 8, and 256 persistent groups, which is why those parameters are explicit in
the general and showcase manifests instead of being hidden code defaults.

## Dense visual stress grid

The final run evaluated 46 stress points and 36 candidates at each point: 1,656
candidate executions. Every candidate produced the correct GPU atlas. Every
point's declared baseline and selected result was stable; noisy comparisons are
automatically rejected and remeasured before artifacts may be emitted.

At a fixed eight scans per plan:

| Elements | Winner | Baseline median | Selected median | Selected P95 | Speedup |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2,097,152 | Wave, group 128, EPT 1 | 0.351490 ms | 0.299623 ms | 0.303142 ms | 1.1731x |
| 4,194,304 | Single-pass, group 512, IPT 8 | 0.531668 ms | 0.441325 ms | 0.444151 ms | 1.2047x |
| 8,388,608 | Single-pass, group 512, IPT 8 | 0.892858 ms | 0.717533 ms | 0.721831 ms | 1.2443x |
| 12,582,912 | Single-pass, group 512, IPT 8 | 2.105765 ms | 1.525680 ms | 1.538930 ms | 1.3802x |

This is the useful boundary: Wave wins the low-pressure 2M row, while
single-pass takes over at 4M and becomes materially stronger once the working
set exceeds the cache-friendly region. The same single-pass configuration wins
the continuous high-pressure band rather than one isolated cell.

## Interpretation and limits

- This result validates one GPU, driver, compiler, and D3D12 queue. The manifest
  must be rerun before using its parameters as a recommendation on another GPU.
- Small jobs still favor Wave because the epoch dispatch, atomics, and look-back
  state have fixed overhead.
- Repeated deterministic input makes this a steady-state workload; it is not a
  standalone DRAM bandwidth specification or an entire application frame.
- The budget animation visualizes measured update cadence, not a quality
  difference. Upload, readback, CPU work, and presentation are outside the GPU
  timestamps.
- RGA was not available in this evidence round. Register pressure and occupancy
  remain intentionally unclaimed rather than inferred.
